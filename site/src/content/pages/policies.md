## Policy governance and review

The directors own every policy in this document. Alastair Driver is responsible for keeping them up to date.

- We review all policies together every year on 1 November, and sooner if the law changes, a client asks, something goes wrong, or the business changes (for example, taking on staff).
- Each review records the date, what changed and who approved it, in the change log at the end of this document.
- Anyone who works for us is given these policies at the start of their engagement and told when they change.
- Breaches of any policy are taken seriously and may lead to disciplinary action or the end of a contract.

## Environmental and carbon reduction policy

We are a small digital business with no premises beyond a home office, so our footprint comes mainly from energy, equipment, cloud services and travel. We aim to keep it low and to keep reducing it.

**What we do**

- **Energy.** Choose renewable electricity tariffs where we have the choice, and switch off equipment when it is not in use.
- **Equipment.** Buy durable hardware, keep it in service as long as it is reliable, and repair before replacing. Old equipment is reused, sold or recycled through a certified electrical waste (WEEE) scheme, after secure data wiping.
- **Travel.** Meet online by default. Where travel is needed, prefer rail and public transport over driving, and avoid flights within Great Britain.
- **Digital work.** Design and build lightweight software: efficient code, compressed media, and cloud resources sized to need and switched off when unused. Prefer hosting and cloud providers that publish carbon commitments.
- **Paper.** Work digitally and print only when necessary.

**Identifying and managing environmental risks**

At each annual review we list the environmental impacts of our work, rate them, and agree actions for the most significant. On client projects we flag any environmental risks we notice and follow the client's environmental requirements.

**Annual reporting**

Each year on 1 November we record an estimate of our carbon footprint (energy, travel and cloud use), the actions taken that year and the targets for the next. The first year is our baseline, and we set a reduction target after it. The record is kept with this document and is available to clients on request.

## Procurement policy

We buy goods and services in a way that is fair, gives good value, and meets our legal, ethical and environmental standards.

- **Need first.** Buy only what we need; reuse or extend what we have where possible.
- **Choosing suppliers.** For anything recurring or over £1,000 a year, compare at least two options on cost, quality, security, data protection, reliability and environmental record.
- **Standards.** Suppliers must meet the standards in our anti-bribery, anti-slavery, data protection and information security policies. Software and cloud services that handle client data must offer appropriate security and UK GDPR terms.
- **No conflicts.** Directors declare any personal interest in a supplier before buying from them.
- **Records.** We keep a supplier register of our regular suppliers (see the supplier management policy).

## Business continuity and disaster recovery plan

Our aim is to keep client work going, or restore it quickly, if something goes wrong.

**Main risks and how we handle them**

| Risk | How we reduce it | How we recover |
|---|---|---|
| Illness or absence of a director | Two directors who can each handle client communication and invoicing. Work is documented and kept in shared, version-controlled repositories. | The other director tells the client the same day, agrees a revised plan, and brings in a vetted associate if the client agrees. |
| Loss, theft or failure of a laptop | Full-disk encryption; no work stored only on one device; work kept in version control and cloud storage. | Revoke the device's access, set up a replacement from cloud and repository backups, and be working again within one working day. |
| Loss of a cloud service or account | Use established providers; keep code in version control with an off-site copy; keep exports of key data. | Restore from the copies, or move to an alternative provider. |
| Cyber incident | The information security policy. | Follow the incident steps in the information security and data protection policies. |
| Loss of home office, power or internet | Laptops, mobile data and alternative places to work. | Work from another location the same day. |

**Testing.** We check once a year that we can restore work from our backups, and we update this plan at the annual review.

**Clients.** If an incident may affect a client's project or data, we tell the client's contact promptly and agree next steps with them.

## Information security policy

We protect our own and our clients' information from loss, theft and misuse.

**Devices**

- Full-disk encryption on all laptops and phones used for work, with a password or biometric lock and automatic screen lock.
- Operating systems, browsers and software kept up to date, with automatic updates on.
- Only supported, legitimate software is installed.

**Accounts and access**

- A password manager, and a unique strong password for every account.
- Multi-factor authentication on every account that supports it, especially email, code hosting, cloud and finance.
- Access to client systems and data limited to what the work needs, and removed at the end of the project.
- Secrets such as API keys and passwords are never put in code repositories, documents or chat.

**Data**

- Client data is stored only in the client's systems or in agreed, secure services, and is deleted or returned at the end of the project as the contract requires.
- Prototypes use sample or anonymised data unless the client agrees otherwise in writing.
- Backups of our own work are kept, and restores are tested once a year.

**Incidents**

If a device is lost, an account may be compromised, or data may have been exposed, the person who notices tells a director immediately. We contain the problem (for example by revoking access and changing credentials), assess what was affected, and tell any affected client without undue delay and within 24 hours. Personal data breaches are handled under the data protection policy.

**AI tools.** Client confidential information is only used with AI tools and services that the client has approved and whose terms protect that data.

## Subcontractor selection and due diligence

We deliver work ourselves. If we ever need a subcontractor, we use one only with the client's agreement and only after these checks.

**Before appointing**

- Confirm identity and company status (for example, Companies House and VAT records).
- Check relevant experience, references and examples of work.
- Check insurance appropriate to the work.
- Check they will meet our policies on data protection, information security, anti-bribery, anti-slavery and tax evasion, and ask for their own policies where relevant.
- Check for conflicts of interest.

**Appointing**

Every subcontractor works under a written agreement that passes on the relevant terms of our client contract, including confidentiality, data protection, intellectual property and the client's own requirements. We remain responsible to the client for their work.

**During and after**

We review their work and conduct during the project. We record the checks we made in the supplier register.

## Supplier management and monitoring

We keep track of who we rely on, and check they remain suitable.

- **Supplier register.** We keep a register of regular suppliers and any subcontractors. It records what they provide, any client data they handle, contract and renewal dates, the checks made, and their own key suppliers where relevant to client work.
- **Annual review.** Each year on 1 November we review each supplier's performance, cost, security and data protection position, and financial stability where we depend on them, and record the outcome.
- **Acting on problems.** If a supplier has a security incident, a change of ownership, financial difficulty or poor performance, we assess the impact on clients and replace the supplier if needed.

## Anti-facilitation of tax evasion policy

Pebble Interactive has zero tolerance of tax evasion, and of anyone facilitating it, in the UK or elsewhere. This reflects Part 3 of the Criminal Finances Act 2017.

- We pay all taxes we owe and keep accurate financial records.
- Nobody working for or with us may help a client, supplier or anyone else evade tax, for example by issuing false invoices, disguising payments, paying cash off the books, or routing payments through third parties to hide them.
- Payments are made only to the person or company that supplied the goods or services, and only against a valid invoice.
- Anyone who suspects tax evasion connected to our work must report it to a director or under the whistleblowing policy.

## Business ethics policy

We work honestly, fairly and openly, and within the law.

- **Honesty.** We describe our experience, capacity and progress accurately, and never misrepresent work or results.
- **Fair dealing.** We compete fairly and do not share pricing or bids with competitors.
- **Conflicts of interest.** We declare any interest that could affect our judgement or a client's interests, and manage or decline the work.
- **Confidentiality.** We protect client confidential information and intellectual property, and use it only for the client's project.
- **Responsible technology.** We design for accessibility and inclusion, avoid manipulative design, and use AI tools responsibly and transparently.
- **Speaking up.** Concerns can be raised under the whistleblowing policy without fear of reprisal.

## Anti-bribery and corruption policy

Pebble Interactive has zero tolerance of bribery and corruption. This policy reflects the Bribery Act 2010.

- **No bribes.** Nobody working for or with us may offer, promise, give, request or accept any payment, gift or advantage to improperly influence a decision, or in return for one.
- **No facilitation payments.** We do not make small unofficial payments to speed up routine processes.
- **Gifts and hospitality.** These are allowed only when modest, open, infrequent and not linked to a decision such as a tender. Anything worth more than £50 is recorded in the gifts and hospitality register and approved by a director. We never offer gifts or hospitality to public officials or to anyone involved in a live procurement with us.
- **Donations.** We make no political donations. Any charitable donation must not be connected to winning or keeping business.
- **Third parties.** Subcontractors and agents acting for us must follow this policy.
- **Records and reporting.** We keep accurate records of payments. Anyone who is asked for a bribe, or suspects bribery, must report it to a director or under the whistleblowing policy.

## Anti-slavery and human trafficking policy

Pebble Interactive is opposed to modern slavery and human trafficking in any form, in our business and our supply chain. This policy reflects the Modern Slavery Act 2015. We are below the turnover threshold for a published statement, but follow these steps voluntarily.

- **Our own people.** Anyone who works for us does so freely, is paid at least the National Living Wage or a fair market rate, and has the right to work in the UK.
- **Our supply chain.** Our supply chain is mainly software, cloud services and IT equipment from established providers. We prefer suppliers that publish modern slavery statements, check new significant suppliers and any subcontractors (see the subcontractor and procurement policies), and will stop working with any supplier found to be involved in modern slavery.
- **Reporting.** Anyone with a concern must report it to a director or under the whistleblowing policy, and we will report credible concerns to the authorities.
- **Review.** We review the risks in our supply chain at each annual review.

## Code of conduct

Everyone who works for or with Pebble Interactive is expected to:

- treat clients, colleagues and the public with respect, and never harass, bully or discriminate;
- act honestly and within the law, and follow these policies and the client's policies;
- protect confidential information, personal data and intellectual property;
- avoid conflicts of interest, and declare any that arise;
- deliver what was agreed, to a professional standard, and say early if something is at risk;
- use company and client equipment, systems and AI tools responsibly and securely;
- represent Pebble and its clients professionally, including on social media, and never share client work without permission;
- raise concerns under the whistleblowing policy.

Breaches may lead to disciplinary action or the end of a contract.

## Data protection policy

We handle personal data lawfully, fairly and securely, in line with the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.

**Our roles**

- **Controller** for our own business data, such as client and supplier contacts and any staff records.
- **Processor** for personal data we handle on a client's behalf. We act only on the client's documented instructions and under a written agreement.

**Principles**

We collect only the personal data we need for a clear, lawful purpose; keep it accurate; keep it only as long as needed; protect it under the information security policy; and are open about how we use it.

**In client work**

- We use sample, synthetic or anonymised data in prototypes wherever possible.
- Real personal data is used only with the client's written agreement and under a data processing agreement.
- We do not transfer personal data outside the UK without appropriate safeguards and the client's agreement.
- We help clients carry out data protection impact assessments where needed.
- At the end of a project we delete or return personal data as the contract requires.

**Individuals' rights**

We respond to requests from individuals (for example, to access or delete their data) within one month, and pass any request about client data to the client straight away.

**Breaches**

Suspected personal data breaches are reported to a director immediately. Where we are processor, we tell the client without undue delay and within 24 hours. Where we are controller, we assess the risk and report to the Information Commissioner's Office within 72 hours where required, and to affected people where there is a high risk.

**ICO fee.** We check each year whether we must pay the ICO data protection fee, and pay it where required.

## Duty of care policy

We take reasonable care of the health, safety and wellbeing of everyone affected by our work, in line with the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974.

- **Working environment.** Home offices and other work places are kept safe, with a suitable workstation set up for display screen use, good lighting and regular breaks.
- **Client sites and events.** We follow the host's health, safety and security rules and inductions.
- **Travel.** Travel is planned to be safe, with reasonable working hours, and avoids driving when overtired.
- **Wellbeing.** We plan workloads realistically, avoid excessive hours, and support anyone who is unwell or struggling.
- **Lone working.** Directors and staff working alone keep the other director informed of their whereabouts when travelling or on site.
- **Reporting.** Accidents, near misses and concerns are reported to a director and recorded, and we act on them.
- **Staff and contractors.** If we take on staff we will carry out written risk assessments, hold employer's liability insurance and provide any training needed.

## Equal opportunities policy

We are committed to equal opportunities and to an inclusive working environment, in line with the Equality Act 2010.

- We do not discriminate against anyone because of age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage or civil partnership, pregnancy or maternity, race, religion or belief, sex or sexual orientation.
- This applies to recruitment, pay, terms, training, promotion and the choice of subcontractors and suppliers.
- We make reasonable adjustments for disabled people, including in recruitment and in how we work with clients.
- We design products that are accessible and inclusive, and work to recognised accessibility standards (WCAG).
- Harassment, bullying and victimisation are not tolerated, and complaints are investigated promptly and fairly.

## Physical security policy

We protect equipment and information from physical loss, theft and unauthorised access.

- **Devices.** Laptops and phones are encrypted, locked when unattended, never left visible in vehicles, and kept with us when travelling.
- **Home office.** Work equipment is kept in a secure home office, and screens are positioned so others cannot read client information.
- **Public places.** Confidential work is not done where it can be overlooked or overheard, and public Wi-Fi is used only with a VPN or a mobile hotspot.
- **Paper.** We avoid printing client information. Any papers are stored securely and shredded when no longer needed.
- **Client premises.** We follow the client's site security rules, including visitor passes and escort requirements.
- **Loss or theft.** Lost or stolen devices are reported to a director immediately and handled under the information security policy.
- **Disposal.** Devices are securely wiped before reuse, sale or recycling.

## Recruitment policy

We do not currently employ staff. When we recruit employees or engage freelance associates, we will do so fairly, lawfully and on merit.

- **Clear roles.** Each role has a written description of the work, skills and terms.
- **Fair selection.** Candidates are assessed against the same criteria by at least one director, using structured interviews or practical tasks related to the work.
- **Equal opportunities.** Recruitment follows the equal opportunities policy, and we offer reasonable adjustments.
- **Checks.** We confirm the right to work in the UK, take references, and carry out further checks where a client requires them.
- **Pay.** We pay at least the National Living Wage and fair market rates.
- **Data.** Candidates' personal data is handled under the data protection policy and deleted when no longer needed.
- **Induction.** New starters are given these policies and an induction.

## Whistleblowing policy

Anyone who works for or with Pebble Interactive can raise concerns about wrongdoing without fear of reprisal. This policy reflects the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998.

**What to report**

For example: crime, fraud, bribery or tax evasion; breaches of the law or of a client contract; dangers to health and safety or the environment; data breaches; modern slavery; or covering up any of these.

**How to report**

- Raise the concern with either director, in person or in writing.
- If the concern involves a director, or you are not comfortable raising it internally, contact the relevant client's procurement or compliance contact, or the relevant prescribed body (such as HMRC, the ICO or the Health and Safety Executive).
- The charity Protect (protect-advice.org.uk) offers free, confidential advice.

**What we will do**

Treat the concern seriously and confidentially; acknowledge it within five working days; look into it and act where needed; and tell the person raising it the outcome, as far as we can. Nobody will be disadvantaged for raising a genuine concern in good faith.
